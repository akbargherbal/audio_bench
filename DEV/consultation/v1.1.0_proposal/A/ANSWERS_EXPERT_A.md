Here are the expert DSP and psychoacoustic diagnoses for the three questions raised regarding the proposed audio analysis extension.

### 1. HNR and Instrumental Bleed: Does bleed depress HNR indistinguishably from vocal noise?

**Diagnosis:** Yes. The proposal’s assumption that HNR strictly proxies "vocal clarity" is fundamentally flawed when applied to imperfectly separated stems.

**Mechanism of Failure:**
Harmonic-to-Noise Ratio (HNR), as computed by Praat/`parselmouth`, relies on an autocorrelation algorithm. It identifies the fundamental period of the signal, extracts the periodic energy (the harmonics), and classifies _all remaining energy_ as aperiodic noise.

If BS-Roformer leaks dense low-mid instrumental content (e.g., synth pads, snare tails, or distorted guitars) into the vocal stem, this leaked audio is not phase-aligned or harmonically related to the vocal's fundamental frequency. Consequently, the autocorrelation function will classify the instrumental bleed entirely as aperiodic noise. Mathematically, the algorithm cannot distinguish between aperiodic energy generated by the glottis (breathiness, vocal fry, distortion) and aperiodic energy introduced by algorithmic bleed. Both will suppress the HNR output (e.g., driving a healthy 20 dB modal phonation down to 8 dB).

**Corrective Intervention:**
You cannot use HNR on a separated stem as a blind proxy for vocal clarity without first validating the isolation quality. To disambiguate genuine vocal noise from instrumental bleed, you must cross-reference HNR with the **Vocal-to-Accompaniment Ratio (VAR)** or compute the spectral flatness of the _instrumental_ stem during the same time window. If the instrumental stem has high transient energy (e.g., percussion) exactly where the vocal stem's HNR drops, the drop is highly likely an artifact of bleed, not a vocal performance issue.

### 2. `librosa.pyin` Pitch Confidence: Does it measure pitch instability or voiced/unvoiced ratio?

**Diagnosis:** The proposal conflates pitch tracking confidence with pitch stability. If unvoiced frames are included in the mean and standard deviation, the resulting statistic describes the **voiced-to-unvoiced ratio (phonation density)**, not pitch quality. The proposed implementation fails to separate these interpretations.

**Mechanism of Failure:**
`librosa.pyin` returns three arrays: `f0`, `voiced_flag`, and `voiced_prob`. The `voiced_prob` (confidence) array represents the _posterior probability that a frame contains a voiced signal_.

During unvoiced consonants (e.g., /s/, /f/, /t/) or breath gaps, the PYIN algorithm correctly drops the confidence to near-zero. Because **Classical Arabic phonology** is highly consonantal and features broadband pharyngeal and uvular fricatives (ع /ʕ/, ح /ħ/, خ /x/), an Arabic vocal track will naturally contain a high percentage of unvoiced or heavily aperiodic frames. If you take the mean of the raw `voiced_prob` array across the whole track, the heavy consonant density will drag the mean down and spike the standard deviation. The LLM will falsely interpret this as "erratic pitch" when it is actually just accurate Arabic pronunciation.

**Corrective Intervention:**
To measure actual pitch instability, you must mask the confidence array using the boolean `voiced_flag`.

1. **For Pitch Tracking Confidence:** Compute the mean and std dev of `voiced_prob` _only_ where `voiced_flag == True`. If confidence is low _during voiced frames_, you have genuine pitch ambiguity (e.g., heavy vibrato crossing bin boundaries, or aliasing).
2. **For Pitch Stability (Melodic Drift):** Do not use `voiced_prob` at all. Compute the variance or derivative of the filled `f0` track (again, only during voiced frames) to measure how wildly the actual pitch is swinging.

### 3. Vocal-to-Accompaniment Ratio (VAR): Can it be computed from fractional band energies?

**Diagnosis:** No. Computing a ratio from the fractional values returned by `_band_energy_fraction()` is mathematically invalid for determining mix balance. It will produce a meaningless number that describes relative EQ, not relative loudness.

**Mechanism of Failure:**
The existing `_band_energy_fraction()` function normalizes the band power by the _total power of that specific stem_.

- Let’s say the vocal stem is a whisper (very low absolute RMS) but 80% of its energy is in the 1k–4kHz presence band. Its fractional value is `0.80`.
- Let's say the instrumental stem is a roaring heavy metal track (very high absolute RMS), but only 20% of its energy is in the 1k–4kHz band. Its fractional value is `0.20`.

If you compute VAR by dividing these fractions (`0.80 / 0.20`), the metric will claim the vocal is dominating the instrumental by a factor of 4. In acoustic reality, the vocal is completely buried. Fractional energy measures the _spectral distribution_ (shape) of a signal, destroying absolute amplitude information in the process.

**Corrective Intervention:**
To compute a meaningful dB-style VAR, you must bypass the fractional normalization entirely. You must compute the **absolute power** (or absolute RMS) in the 1k–4kHz band for both stems independently, and then take the logarithmic ratio:

`VAR_dB = 10 * np.log10( absolute_vocal_band_power / absolute_inst_band_power )`

This requires writing a new helper function (e.g., `_band_absolute_power()`) that omits the `/ total_power` division step found in the current `extractor.py`.
